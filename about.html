About Ember & Olive

A restaurant built the slow way — around a wood fire, a short list of ingredients, and a room designed for people to actually talk to each other.

The story

It started with one hearth and a short list of rules.

Ember & Olive opened in New York with a simple premise: every dish passes near real fire before it reaches the table. No shortcuts, no gas-range substitutes for the char that only wood can give. What began as a single hearth in a small space has grown into a full dining room, but the rules from day one haven't changed.

Philosophy

Three things we don't compromise on

Real fire

Every hot dish touches the wood hearth at some point in its cooking — it's slower, and the flavor isn't replicable any other way.

Known sources

We can name the farm, boat, or producer behind almost everything on the menu. If we can't, it doesn't go on the plate.

Unhurried service

Tables are booked with room to breathe. We'd rather serve fewer covers well than rush a room.

In the kitchen

A kitchen led from the hearth outward.

Our kitchen team trained across wood-fire kitchens on both coasts before bringing that approach to Ember & Olive. The pass is arranged so every station can see the fire — it sets the pace for the whole night, and every dish is built to finish near it.

Ingredients

The menu changes because the season does.

We rebuild parts of the menu as often as every few weeks, following what our growers are actually harvesting rather than working around a fixed list.

Atmosphere

A room built for staying a while.

Low lighting, oak tables, and a soundtrack kept low enough to hear the person across from you — the dining room is designed to slow a night down, not move it along.
